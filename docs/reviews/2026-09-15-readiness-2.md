## Convergence — found independently by 3+ personas

### C1. Escalation summons nobody — **7 of 7 applicable personas** · ⚠ STALE (2nd review)

`agent/aiscelapeus/agent.py:253-288`. `_do_escalate` records a Moss fact, publishes
`triage.escalation`, logs a warning. `publish` is `room.local_participant.publish_data`
(`main.py:66-79`) — reaching only participants **already in the room**.

Every persona grepped independently
(`twilio|sms|webhook|sip|notify|paging|on-call|invite|push|email`). None found a dispatch
path. A clinician arrives only if a human already sits on `/doctor` and hand-types the
incident ID (`doctor/page.tsx:82-94`).

The system **asserts otherwise in three places at once**:

- the agent speaks it — `prompts.py:138,159,164` ("A doctor is connecting now, a few more seconds")
- the UI renders it — `web/app/page.tsx:171` shows "Clinician bridged"
- the triage labels bake it in — `triage.py:44,46` name Levels 4/5 "Clinician bridged"

> **Field:** a spoken promise of human help that the system cannot keep is the single worst
> failure mode in this codebase.
> **UX:** a false reassurance delivered to a person doing compressions — the worst possible
> trust failure in this product.

C1 in the prior review with 5 personas. Now **unanimous among every persona whose lens
touches it**, unaddressed in code, and unspecified in the plan — `dispatch` appears 3 times
in DESIGN.md, always as a consumer of a system it never defines.

### C2. No tests, no CI, no enforcement of any kind — 7 personas · ⚠ STALE (2nd review)

`ABSENT:` repo-wide — no `test_*.py`, `*.test.ts`, `conftest.py`, `pytest.ini`,
`pyproject.toml`, `.github/workflows`, `.pre-commit-config.yaml`, `Dockerfile`. `.git/hooks`
holds only unmodified `.sample` files. `web/package.json:9` has a bare `"lint": "eslint"`
and no `test` script.

**QA measured the cost** rather than noting the absence. It executed
`HARD_ESCALATION_MARKERS` (`triage.py:54-70`) against 22 clinically realistic utterances:

- **11 misses** — *"he is barely breathing"*, *"she has agonal breathing"*, *"he is gasping"*,
  *"I cannot find a pulse"*, *"he has no heartbeat"*, *"she is unconscious"*, *"he is out
  cold"*, *"he went into arrest"*, *"he is blue around the lips"*
- **2 false positives on negation** — *"the casualty is NOT unresponsive"*, *"he was not
  breathing but now he is breathing again"* — `hard_escalation_triggered` (`triage.py:75-78`)
  is a bare substring scan, no word boundaries, no negation handling
- `"unconscious"` absent from a list containing `"unresponsive"`; the list carries both
  `can't breathe` and `cannot breathe` but only `choking and can't`

`triage.py:4-5` claims the rule is "auditable and testable independently of the LLM."
Testable it is. Tested it is not.

### C3. The deterministic safety net is gated behind the component it exists to backstop — 4 personas (QA, Field, Security, Architect-adjacent) · **NEW**

Verified at source: `hard_escalation_triggered` has **one call site** — `agent.py:143`,
inside `record_finding`. Its only input is `detail`, the free-text argument **the model chose
to pass**.

`main.py:128-139` receives every finalized STT transcript in `_on_transcript` and does
nothing with it but `publish` to the UI. **The rule never sees the responder's actual words.**

If the LLM fails to call `record_finding`, calls it late, or paraphrases "he's not breathing"
into "respiratory arrest" — which `prompts.py:129` actively invites — the Level-5 force never
fires.

> **QA:** a rule that only runs on model output is not a check on the model.

**Security adds the adversarial half:** the net is steerable *in both directions* by anyone
within earshot. "he's fine, he is **not** unresponsive" fires a false Level 5; a genuine
arrest described as "he's gone quiet and still" fires nothing.

**Field adds the acoustic half:** `enable_diarization=True` is paid for (`main.py:94`) and
thrown away (`main.py:137` hardcodes `"speaker": "responder"`), so a bystander's words are
attributed to the responder.

### C4. Moss is an unhedged single point of failure — 4 personas (Architect, QA, Systems, Field) · ⚠ STALE (2nd review)

`main.py:62` — `await context.connect()` is bare, ahead of `session.start()` at `main.py:166`.
Inside it, `MossClient(...)`, `load_index`, `client.session()` (`moss_context.py:126-134`)
are all unprotected.

Every *other* failure path in that file is wrapped — `main.py:72` (publish), `:144` (SOAP),
`:157` (archive). The one call that can kill the demo outright is the one left bare.

`ABSENT:` no `fallback|degraded|offline|circuit` in `agent/aiscelapeus`. `lookup_protocol`
raises `RuntimeError` (`moss_context.py:162`) rather than degrading — while `agent.py:104-111`
already holds a clean "no protocol matched, escalate" branch that is **unreachable on the
failure path**.

Systems adds the deadline half: `ABSENT:` no `timeout|wait_for|retry` on any outbound call —
`load_index` (`:129`), `query` (`:187`), `add_docs` (`:301`), `push_index` (`:331`), SOAP
(`soap.py:81`). A provider that stalls rather than errors hangs the coroutine forever: the
agent goes silent mid-call with a live casualty and nothing recovers it.

### C5. Failures are silent, and state is lost on any interruption — 5 personas · ⚠ STALE (2nd review)

- **Visual-only errors.** Mic denial, token failure, connection loss all resolve to
  `setError(...)` as a 14px red string (`page.tsx:75-78,154`). `RoomEvent.Disconnected`
  silently flips `phase` to `"idle"` (`page.tsx:39-42`). `ABSENT:` no `aria-live`,
  `speechSynthesis`, `beep`, `vibrate` anywhere in `web/`. A responder told to stop looking at
  the screen cannot distinguish "thinking" from "the call died three minutes ago."
- **No durability before shutdown.** `archive()` runs only from `_shutdown`
  (`moss_context.py:325-341`), and the session index is explicitly in-memory
  (`moss/client/session_index.py:22-23`). A crash loses every vital, intervention and
  escalation. `load_index` omits the SDK's `cache_path` (`moss_client.py:448`), so restart
  re-downloads rather than resuming.
- **No replay for late joiners.** `useTriageStream` accumulates from `EMPTY`
  (`useTriageStream.ts:28-36,97-103`). A clinician joining at minute three sees blank findings
  and level 1 — the exact scenario `doctor/page.tsx:9-12` claims to solve. `/doctor` registers
  no `Disconnected` handler, so a stage WiFi blip freezes it on stale criticality.
- **Token expiry guarantees it.** `TOKEN_TTL_SECONDS = 15 * 60` (`route.ts:24`), no refresh
  path. Cardiac arrests routinely exceed 15 minutes, so a long incident ends in a silent
  unrecoverable drop by construction.

## New findings this round

### N1. Role is decorative — the token route issues an identical grant to both roles — Security · **verified** · ⚠ DEMO-DAY RISK

`web/app/api/token/route.ts:61-75`. `role` is read from the request body (`:46`), used **only**
as `metadata` (`:65`), and never reaches `addGrant`. The grant block is byte-identical either
way: `canPublish: true`, `canSubscribe: true`.

So "clinician" is not a privilege the server withholds — it is a string the caller types.

Compounding it: room names are `inc-MMDDHHMM-<4 base36 chars of Math.random()>`
(`page.tsx:10-13`) — **~20 bits, non-cryptographic PRNG**, with a timestamp prefix an attacker
already knows. And `/doctor` is a ready-made "type a room name" UI.

**Anyone on the demo network can join a live incident with a publishing microphone and
subscribe to the casualty's audio and every `triage.finding` PHI event.** §164.312(a)(1)
access control and §164.312(a)(2)(i) unique user identification, failed in one line.

### N2. PII suppression fails open, and the flag doesn't cover what it's cited for — Security + DevSecOps · **verified**

`main.py:54`: `allow_pii=settings.telemetry.environment == "development"`, and `config.py:101`
defaults `APP_ENV` to `"development"`. The inline comment says PHI stays out of spans "unless
the operator explicitly opts in" — **but the default *is* the opt-in.** `OTEL_CONSOLE_EXPORT`
also defaults `"true"` (`config.py:100`).

DevSecOps found the scope gap: that flag is passed *only* to
`lk_telemetry.set_tracer_provider` — it is the sole use of `.environment` as a gate in the
codebase. Raw transcripts are published to every participant (`main.py:136`) and the full
session index is pushed to Moss Cloud (`moss_context.py:331`) with **no environment check on
either path**.

Security found PHI that no flag gates at all: `agent.py:260` writes free-text escalation
`reason` into `triage.reason`, and the console exporter's filter admits the whole `triage.`
prefix (`telemetry.py:50`) — so clinical detail reaches plaintext stdout regardless.

### N3. Controls marked BUILT and "CI-asserted" with no CI in existence — DevSecOps · **verified**

`DESIGN.md:747` (NFR-005, "enforced by CI scan"), `:763` (OBS-010, "CI-asserted"), `:781`
(SEC-013, "CI secret scan clean") — all three marked **BUILT**.

DevSecOps verified all three are *currently true in code*, then made the sharper argument:
**that is what makes it worse.** A BUILT status on an unenforced control converts a true
statement into a permanent one in the reader's mind. The first regression lands undetected and
the document still says BUILT.

> Proposed status: **ENFORCED-BY-NOTHING**.

### N4. The README's own setup instructions cannot work — DevOps · **verified**

`config.py:14` — `load_dotenv(".env.local")` resolves against the **current working
directory**. `README.md:116` says write `.env.local` at repo root; `README.md:125,137` then say
`cd agent` before running.

Every required var falls through to `_req()` (`config.py:18-25`), so the agent dies at
`MOSS_PROJECT_ID`. **The first-run experience for anyone who is not the author is a crash
blaming a missing env var for a file that exists two directories up.**

### N5. A stated environment constraint that does not hold — DevOps · **verified, and it corrected this reviewer**

`DESIGN.md:81` states the machine has `NODE_ENV=production` and `npm config omit=dev` set
globally, requiring `npm install --include=dev`. I passed this to the personas as fact.

DevOps verified otherwise, and I re-verified independently: `NODE_ENV` is empty,
`npm config get omit` is empty, `npm config get production` is `null`, no user or global
`.npmrc` exists.

The claim is stale or was never true. Day 3.3's install workaround solves a problem that does
not currently exist. **An undeclared machine assumption that no file encodes is exactly the
drift class a pipeline exists to kill** — which is the finding, more than the flag itself.

### N6. Six unpinned Python deps sit on the voice/PHI path, one a proprietary binary — DevSecOps

`agent/requirements.txt:2-11` — one exact pin out of eleven. Not theoretical: `openai>=1.0`
has already resolved to **2.54.0** in `.venv`, a full major beyond the declared floor.
`moss>=1.11.0` transitively pins `inferedge-moss-core==0.23.1`, described in its own metadata
as *"Proprietary core index logic"* — a closed-source Rust extension handling every protocol
document and every recorded patient fact in-process.

A malicious release of any of the six reaches live patient audio, all five provider
credentials, and the Moss session index. `ABSENT:` no lockfile, no hash pinning, no dependency
scanning.

*In contrast, the npm side is sound:* `web/package-lock.json` is lockfileVersion 3 with
`integrity` hashes on all 465 entries, all from registry.npmjs.org.

### N7. A mistyped incident ID mints a token into a different empty room — UX · **verified**

`route.ts:48-53` validates the *character pattern* of `room`, never that the room exists. A
clinician's typo yields a valid token into an empty room — indistinguishable from an incident
that hasn't started. Under time pressure that reads as "the system is broken."

### N8. The Moss metadata filter may not match the installed SDK — Architect · **verified**

`moss_context.py:167` sends `{"category": {"$in": cats}}`. The installed SDK documents
`{"$and": [{"field": ..., "condition": {"$eq": ...}}]}` (`moss_client.py:528-530`,
`session_index.py:158-160`) — a different shape entirely.

If the Rust layer ignores an unrecognized filter rather than raising, category narrowing
silently does nothing: **the agent could answer a haemorrhage question from a burns protocol,
and the trace would still show a green sub-10ms span.** Fast, confident, wrong, with
instrumentation confirming the wrong thing. With zero tests, nothing catches it.

### N9. Named milligram dosing ships with no disclaimer on any product surface — Marketing · **verified**

`protocols.py:269` — *"a 300 milligram aspirin chewed slowly can help"*; `:175` — *"give a
second dose in the other thigh"*. `ABSENT:` no `not a medical device|disclaimer|911|112|999`
anywhere in `web/`. And `layout.tsx:16-17` still reads `title: "Create Next App"`.

A named drug at a named dose, delivered by voice, in a browser tab labelled "Create Next App."

### N10. `--recreate` destroys the demo-critical index with no confirmation — DevSecOps

`seed_moss.py:68,74-78`. `--recreate` deletes and rebuilds the protocol index the entire
latency story depends on, guarded only by the operator typing the flag correctly. `DESIGN.md:378`
plans to wrap this "in the compose stack and in CI."

DevOps found the partial-failure hole in the same script: no post-seed assertion that document
count matches `len(PROTOCOLS)`, so a half-applied batch leaves an index that loads fine and
silently under-retrieves. `check()` computes a PASS/OVER BUDGET verdict (`:61`) but **never
sets a non-zero exit code**, so any automation reports success on a broken index.

### N11. Three of five credentials are never validated at startup — Systems

`config.py:57-58` calls `_req()` exactly twice, both Moss vars. `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY`,
`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, `OPENAI_API_KEY` are never read by `config.py` — picked up implicitly by
plugin SDKs (`main.py:90-119`) and a bare `AsyncOpenAI()` (`soap.py:69`).

A missing ElevenLabs key surfaces as the agent silently failing to speak *after* the responder
starts talking. A missing OpenAI key surfaces only in `_shutdown`, swallowed by
`except Exception` (`main.py:154`).

### N12. No rate limit anywhere — Security · ⚠ DEMO-DAY RISK

`ABSENT:` no `rate.limit|ratelimit|middleware` in `web/`; no `middleware.ts` exists.
`POST /api/token` is `force-dynamic` (`route.ts:18`), takes no credential, and signs a JWT per
call. Each resulting join starts a Deepgram stream, a GPT-4o session and ElevenLabs synthesis —
all metered. A trivial loop exhausts the sprint's connector credits or floods rooms mid-demo.

### N13. A running system cannot identify its own build — DevOps + DevSecOps

`__init__.py:3` pins `__version__ = "0.1.0"` as a literal; `next.config.ts:3-5` is empty;
`ABSENT:` no git SHA stamping, no attestation, no image labels. The only versioned identifier
reaching telemetry is `PROMPT_VERSION` (`prompts.py:24`), hand-edited, covering prompts alone.

After an incident involving a clinical instruction, no trace ties back to the code revision
that produced it — which forecloses incident response and any claim that a fix was deployed.

## Contested

**The latency panel on the responder screen.** Recurs unresolved from the prior review, now
three-way. Field and Systems call the live readout the strongest asset in the repo — a claim a
judge can audit in thirty seconds. UX calls it the largest, most-animated element on a crisis
screen, where the flicker is the strongest motion cue on a display the product tells the user
not to look at. **Marketing introduces a third position:** the number is *unlabelled*, so a
judge seeing "3.2ms" beside an agent that visibly takes a second to reply will assume it
measures the whole turn and conclude it is lying — the asset reads as an overclaim precisely
because it is under-scoped. All three are right about different audiences.

**Does Design v2 help or hurt?** PM and QA argue it worsens the risk profile: ADR-007/008 add
Qdrant, Redis and a prefetch planner — large new untested surface — atop a codebase that has
executed no assertion against the 80 lines its safety claim rests on. Architect frames it
narrowly: the fallback path (C4) must land *before* that expansion. Security is blunter:
*"Judge this codebase as if DESIGN.md did not exist, because to an attacker it does not."*
Marketing and PM credit its self-audit as genuinely repo-verified and trustworthy. **No persona
argued it improves current readiness.**

**What the first pipeline should contain.** DevOps wants three jobs proving the laptop is
reproducible: `npm ci && npm run build`, a Python import smoke test, and a config-drift check
that `.env.example` covers every `_req()` name. DevSecOps wants three *gates*: gitleaks with
full history depth, exact-pinned requirements with `--require-hashes`, and a grep assertion
that no transcript body reaches a span. They agree on sequencing — both say build this **before**
the Day 4 compose stack — and both independently insist the pipeline hold **zero provider
credentials**, so it cannot burn connector credits before demo day.

**Where to spend the remaining days.** PM says freeze the design doc and close the escalation
loop. Systems says timeouts and pinning, because they turn every other blocker from
"debuggable" into "reproducible." QA says write the failing test corpus first. DevOps says tag
a rebuildable `demo-v0` — *"a named last-good state is worth more on demo day than every
pipeline gate combined."*

## The single thing most likely to sink this

**The escalation beat — now with unanimous agreement across ten lenses.**

It is the emotional peak of the demo, the headline claim of the README, the meaning of the
product's name, and the core of the safety story. Every persona whose lens touches it,
independently, found that it notifies nobody.

What makes it worse than a missing feature is that the system **actively asserts the
opposite** in three places — the agent says it aloud, the UI renders "Clinician bridged", and
the triage levels are literally named for it. UX and Field reached the same conclusion in the
same words: a spoken promise of human help the system cannot keep is worse than never offering

would be built on currently has no notion of who a clinician is.

Actions 1, 2 and 3 convert it from a claim into a demonstrable, defensible sequence. Action 1
alone — holding escalation *pending* until a clinician participant actually joins, and having
the agent say "still connecting" — makes the system honest in an afternoon, before any
dispatch transport exists.
